A mocha lives or dies on its chocolate. Most versions drown the espresso in syrup until the coffee is just a rumor somewhere underneath. This one goes the other way: a dark, roasty blend that already leans chocolate, and just enough real sauce to meet it halfway.

The sauce came from a local vendor who makes it in small batches with actual cocoa rather than the shelf-stable squeeze-bottle stuff. It's thicker and less sweet than commercial syrup, which turns out to be exactly what a mocha wants. You taste cocoa first, sugar second.

## The Beans

[Forty-Six](/beans/counter-culture-forty-six) is Counter Culture's darkest offering, and it's built for a drink like this. The roast brings smoke, molasses, and a bittersweet chocolate depth that doesn't disappear behind milk. Where a lighter roast would get lost between the sauce and the foam, Forty-Six holds the low end of the cup like a bass line.

If you're working with a different bean, aim dark. Fruity, delicate roasts fight with chocolate; roasty, syrupy ones lock into it.

## Pulling the Shot

Standard recipe here: 18g in, 36g out, right around 28 seconds. The 1:2 ratio keeps the shot concentrated enough to cut through both the milk and the chocolate.

I pull the shot directly onto the sauce, about a tablespoon in the bottom of a warmed cup. The heat of the espresso melts it completely, and a quick stir with a small spoon gives you a smooth chocolate-espresso base before any milk touches it. If you add the sauce after the milk, you'll find sludge at the bottom of the cup. Melt it first.

## Steaming the Milk

Whole milk, 60°C. Chocolate mutes foam texture a little, so I stretch slightly less than I would for a cappuccino. The sauce adds body on its own, and too much air makes the drink feel like a milkshake.

The chocolate base actually makes latte art easier in one way: the canvas is darker, so the contrast pops. The rosetta on top of this one settled into the crema better than it usually does on a plain latte.

## Assembly

1. Warm the cup, add about a tablespoon of chocolate sauce
2. Pull the double shot directly over the sauce and stir until fully melted
3. Steam whole milk to 60°C with slightly less air than a cappuccino
4. Pour from low and let the art come through
5. Resist the urge to add more sauce on top. The balance is already there

## Notes

The vendor's sauce is noticeably less sweet than syrup, so if you substitute a commercial one, cut the quantity by a third or the drink tips into hot-chocolate territory. And drink it warm rather than hot. As it cools a few degrees, the chocolate and the molasses note in the Forty-Six merge into something surprisingly close to a dark chocolate bar.
