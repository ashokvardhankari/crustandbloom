This loaf started as a pantry experiment: a jar of sun-dried tomatoes, a bottle of Trader Joe's Italian spice mix, and a batch of dough that needed a purpose. It's now one of the loaves I get asked for most.

The tomatoes bring the sweetness and acidity, and their oil swirls through the dough during lamination, streaking the crumb a deep orange. The spice mix does the rest. One bottle gets you garlic, herbs and seasoning in a single scoop, already balanced, so there's no measuring out five separate jars. A little goes inside the dough and a little goes on top, where it toasts in the oven.

## Formula

| Ingredient | Weight | Baker's % |
|---|---|---|
| Bread flour | 450g | 90% |
| Whole wheat flour | 50g | 10% |
| Water | 380g | 76% |
| Starter (active) | 100g | 20% |
| Fine sea salt | 9g | 1.8% |
| **Sun-dried tomatoes in oil, chopped** | **80g** | - |
| **Trader Joe's Italian spice mix** | **2 tbsp** | - |

Quantities are per loaf. Salt drops slightly to 1.8% because the spice mix is already seasoned and the tomatoes carry salt from their brine.

## On the Inclusions

**Sun-dried tomatoes.** Use the oil-packed kind. Drain them but don't pat them dry: the clinging oil is what streaks the crumb orange and keeps the pieces soft. Chop into pieces roughly the size of your fingernail. Large strips tear the dough during shaping, and anything left on the surface will scorch into black, chewy edges (some of that is part of the charm).

**Italian spice mix.** Two tablespoons inside the dough gives a clear herb flavour without tipping into bitterness. Keep an extra teaspoon or two for the top. If you can't get to a Trader Joe's, any Italian seasoning blend with dried garlic works; add a pinch of garlic powder if yours is herbs only.

## Mix and Bulk Fermentation

Mix the flours with 360g of the water and autolyse for 30 to 45 minutes. Add the starter, salt, and remaining 20g of water and work them in until the dough is smooth and cohesive.

About 30 minutes into bulk, laminate. Wet your counter, stretch the dough into a thin rectangle, then scatter the spice mix evenly over the surface and dot the tomatoes across it. Fold the dough back up into a package and return it to the bowl.

Follow with three sets of coil folds, 30 minutes apart, then leave the dough to finish bulk. It runs about 5 hours total at 76°F. Look for a 50 to 60% rise, a domed surface and bubbles along the sides. The dough will turn steadily more orange as the tomato oil works through it.

## Shaping

Pre-shape gently and bench rest 20 minutes. Final shape into a boule or batard, tucking any exposed tomato pieces underneath so they don't burn.

Cold retard 10 to 14 hours.

## Scoring and Baking

Preheat the Dutch oven at 500°F for 45 minutes.

Turn the loaf out, mist the top lightly with water, and sprinkle a teaspoon or two of the spice mix over it. Then dust with flour and score. A wheat-stalk pattern, a centre line with angled cuts down each side, shows off the contrast between the dusted crust and the opened scores.

Bake covered at 500°F for 20 minutes, then uncover, drop to 450°F, and bake another 20 to 25 minutes, until deep golden. Tomato pieces at the surface will blacken in spots before the rest of the crust is done; tent with foil if they're going too far.

Cool at least 2 hours before cutting.

## How to Eat It

Thick slices, toasted, with good olive oil and flaky salt. Under a fried egg. As the bread for a grilled cheese with mozzarella and basil. Or cubed and baked into croutons for a Caesar salad, which might be the best use of all.
