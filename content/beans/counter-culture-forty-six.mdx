This is the bag that's almost always open on my counter. Forty-Six is Counter Culture's darkest offering, a year-round espresso blend built to be consistent and forgiving across a range of home setups. That's exactly what I want from a coffee I reach for every morning.

## What the bag says vs. what I taste

The bag leans on "smoky" and "intense," and that's fair, but it undersells the sweetness. What I get most consistently is bittersweet chocolate first, then smoke and molasses through the middle, and a dried-fruit sweetness underneath, fig some days, raisin others. When it's dialled in the roast reads as depth rather than burn, with a long, slightly sweet finish.

## How it brews

I pull it at 21g in, 42g out, in about 25 seconds. That's the same 1:2 ratio as a textbook shot, scaled up to stand against the 180ml of milk in my cappuccino. At 18g it gets a little lost under the milk. The extra couple of grams keep the chocolate assertive.

It's forgiving. A few seconds long or short and it's still pleasant, which is more than I can say for the brighter single origins I rotate in.

## The verdict

It's not the most exciting coffee I drink, and that's the point. I want something I can dial once and trust for weeks, and Forty-Six earns that spot. Would I rebuy? I already have, more times than I can count.
